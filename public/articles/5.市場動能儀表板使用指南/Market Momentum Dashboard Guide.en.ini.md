**Assets that performed strongly may continue to lead over the medium term.** Momentum investing starts with this tendency and looks for assets that are already strong and continue to outperform. [Research on stock momentum](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x) documented this effect over holding periods of 3 to 12 months.

The [Market Momentum Dashboard](/en/momentum/) organizes the search into two levels: find strong groups and ETFs, then look for strong stocks among their holdings. ETF investors can start with the first two steps. Continue to the holdings if you want to screen individual stocks.

## Use the screener to narrow your watchlist

Switch to “Stock screener,” then choose the stocks you want to find:

- **Strong candidates:** Find stocks in strong sectors that have recently outperformed the market.
- **Pullback watch · RSI 40–60:** Use this when you want to assess an entry after a strong stock retreats.
- **Oversold watch · RSI <30:** Find stocks with stronger recent selling pressure and watch for the decline to stop.

All three screens first find sector ETFs with momentum scores of at least 80 and continuing recent market outperformance, then screen their stocks. Pullback and oversold watch also check for a price retreat and the selected RSI range.

Results show average daily turnover so you can compare trading activity. Before placing an order, check turnover and the bid–ask spread.

Select “View results” when ready. Pro users can open a stock to review its momentum score, pullback depth and charts, then decide which candidates to follow. Candidate stocks and source ETFs have separate tabs that remember selections and scroll positions. Only ETFs with matching stocks appear in the source tab, with the number of matching constituents. Select a stock’s source ETF to open its details. Free users can apply the same filters and see qualifying counts; upgrading to Pro unlocks the full list and details.

The four steps below take you from sectors to stocks and help you observe possible entry opportunities.

## 1. Find strong groups

Select “Sectors & themes” and “Recent Momentum.” Each category tile shows the average momentum score of its assets. Start with higher-scoring groups. The small line shows the group's average score over the past 60 trading sessions; a rising line means its ranking is improving.

![Market Momentum Dashboard category map showing colors and momentum scores](./image-map-en.jpg)

Select a category to see its ETFs. This reveals which funds are leading and whether a few ETFs account for most of the group's strength.

The other tabs serve different comparisons. “Major assets” covers U.S. and international equities, bonds and commodities. “Market structure” covers market-cap groups, equal-weight funds and stock-selection strategies. ETFs are ranked separately within each tab, so choose a comparison group first.

To see the latest session's moves, switch to “Daily Change.” Category tiles then show the median daily return of their members, and you can sort the asset list by daily change.

## 2. Compare ETFs and check whether they keep leading

In “Market overview,” select the row of a high-scoring ETF within the group. Its details compare performance over 20, 60 and 120 trading sessions, roughly 1, 3 and 6 months.

**The momentum score describes how highly an asset's recent performance ranks.** Assets that rose more, or fell less, score higher. Period returns show the actual gains or losses, so you can see whether a high-scoring fund is rising or losing less in a declining market.

“Versus SPY” shows how far the asset leads or trails the broad market. SPY tracks the S&P 500. For example, if an ETF gained 12% while SPY gained 8% over the same period, it leads by 4 percentage points.

Next, look at “Relative strength versus SPY”:

- **A rising relative strength line:** The asset is outperforming SPY over that interval. A falling line means it is underperforming.
- **A line above a rising 20-session average:** Recent outperformance is continuing, making the asset a candidate to follow.

The line divides the asset's price by SPY's price and starts at 100 on the chart. This lets you check continued market leadership in the same way for assets with different share prices.

## 3. Screen stocks within the ETF

Select “Constituents” in the ETF's details to see stock names, fund weights and rankings. Pro includes “Minimum momentum score” and “Minimum average turnover (USD millions)” filters.

For example, to view stocks with momentum scores of at least 80 and average daily turnover of at least $10 million, enter **80** and **10**. These are example filters; adjust the thresholds to your trading size.

**Average daily turnover** measures trading activity. Higher turnover generally makes a stock easier to trade. The dashboard averages daily closing price times volume over 20 trading sessions. When placing an order, also check the current bid–ask spread and available order sizes.

Stock scores compare SPY constituents and other available equity holdings from the ETFs together. A stock opened from a technology ETF keeps its score within this broader U.S. stock list. The dashboard displays the number of stocks included in the comparison.

Expand a stock to see period returns, its lead or lag versus SPY, and its relative strength line with the 20-session average. This takes the search from a strong group to individual stocks that continue to lead.

Some ETFs currently provide only their top holdings. These lists are marked “partial,” with the share of the fund covered. Holdings dates and price dates are shown separately; missing holdings dates are labeled. The holdings lists currently include USD-denominated equities.

## 4. Use RSI to observe a pullback

Once you have candidates, use **14-session RSI** to observe recent buying and selling strength. RSI ranges from 0 to 100. Higher values indicate stronger recent gains; lower values indicate stronger losses. Readings above 70 are commonly considered overbought, while readings below 30 are considered oversold. [RSI indicator guide](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/technical-analysis/technical-indicator-guide/RSI)

If you prefer to assess an entry during a pullback, watch RSI retreat from higher levels while checking whether the momentum score stays high and the relative strength line remains above a rising average. Together, these measures help assess whether the asset retains its leadership through a short-term pullback.

**Prices can keep falling when RSI is below 30.** In a strong downtrend, RSI can remain low for an extended period. Momentum scores include several months of performance, so a stock may still score highly just after it starts weakening. When reviewing “Oversold watch,” observe whether the price has stopped falling and whether relative strength is continuing to deteriorate before assessing an entry.

Repeat the same screening process as you follow candidates. If the momentum score drops and relative strength continues to weaken, reassess whether the asset belongs on the list. Set an acceptable loss and position size before entering so you have a plan for managing the holding.

## How momentum scores are calculated

The dashboard uses adjusted closing prices to calculate returns over 20, 60 and 120 trading sessions, then converts each period's performance into a 0–100 ranking score. The three periods receive weights of **20%, 40% and 40%**. Greater weight on the longer periods reduces the influence of a single month's moves.

ETFs rank within each tab's watchlist. Stocks rank within the shared U.S. stock list described above. Category tiles average their members' scores. Periods with insufficient data show missing values. Check the dashboard for the latest price date and coverage.
